# Incident Response Standard

Severity 1 incidents must be escalated within 15 minutes to the Security Lead and the CISO. Severity 2 incidents must be escalated within 2 hours. A postmortem is required within 5 business days.

This policy defines the minimum security expectations for third-party vendors that process company data, connect to company systems, or provide services that materially support business operations. Critical vendors require SOC 2 Type II evidence before onboarding and during each annual vendor review. The review examines the current report period, exceptions, complementary user-entity controls, disaster recovery commitments, subprocessors, breach-notification language, and any material changes since the prior review. A vendor may be classified as critical because it stores Restricted information, supports production authentication, handles payment operations, or would create a material outage if unavailable.

Vendor owners must maintain an inventory record with the business purpose, data categories, contract owner, renewal date, and security reviewer. The inventory is reviewed at least annually so the company can identify services that changed scope, expanded integrations, or no longer have a valid business owner. Contracts must document confidentiality, encryption expectations, incident notification, return or destruction of company information, and offboarding duties. Offboarding must be completed within a 30-day window after the business relationship ends, unless legal retention duties require a narrower data-preservation exception approved by Security and Legal.

Security reviewers evaluate whether a vendor uses multi-factor authentication for administrative access, separates customer environments, encrypts sensitive data in transit and at rest, and maintains reasonable backup and recovery controls. High-risk findings require a remediation plan, an accountable owner, and a due date. If remediation cannot be completed before use, the requesting department must document compensating controls and obtain risk acceptance from the Security Lead. Risk acceptance is not a permanent waiver; it expires at the next annual vendor review or sooner if circumstances change.

Procurement teams may not treat a sales questionnaire as a substitute for evidence. Marketing claims, screenshots, and unsupported attestations are helpful context but not sufficient for critical vendors. SOC 2 Type II reports, ISO certificates, penetration-test summaries, and independent audit letters are preferred because they give reviewers verifiable evidence. When a report contains exceptions, reviewers should ask whether the issue affects the specific service in scope, whether remediation is complete, and whether customers need to implement complementary controls.

Vendor reviews must be recorded with the evidence examined, decisions made, open risks, and next review date. The review record should allow another analyst to reconstruct the decision without relying on memory. Vendor relationships that involve Restricted data, privileged access, or material operational dependency require closer monitoring because the blast radius of failure is larger. This policy is intended to improve judgment, not replace it; reviewers should escalate unusual facts, missing evidence, or inconsistent vendor statements for validation.
